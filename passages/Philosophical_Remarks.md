# Philosophical Remarks

## On Democritus

Friendship in Democritus’ atomism functions as a very critical supplement to the **normative defficiency**(or, put in a better way, voidness) of his physics, which is notable when applied to the ethical/political sphere. Voidness/not-being is the only _explanatum_  of atoms' eternal movements(“change”), collision with each other(“encounter”), their entangling and grasping hold(“congregation”), and also,  being brought apart by a stronger force(“segregation”).[pp.206-7] Given that for those early thinkers physics always aligns with their moral opinions, this radicalized physics of atomism can somehow result in a ‘voidness’ in ethics. On this point, Simplicius‘ well-known commentary is fair enough. Through rejecting any form of teleology and certainty — the only reality being “atoms and the empty”[B125, p.209] — the segregation or congregation of beings is attributed to mere “spontaneity and chance, ”[Ibid.] for they’re essentially the effect of voidness alike. In a more pratical sense, the vague borderline between segregation and congregation means nothing more than this: the necessity/unnecessity of gathering in a  community is suspicious.
Therefore, the emphasis of friendship provides an **immanent motivation**, in a very moderate way, for the collision and encounter of beings. “A man who has not a single good friend does not deserve to live.”[B99, p.252] Here, Democritus binds the engagement of friendship — another congregation form of human beings in addition to kinship mentioned somewhere else[B107, p.252] — and the meaning of life, which functions as the immanent reason for each individual to get into the community. However, apart from friendship, Democritus’ picture of congregation is more complicated than we might think. The fortune [B106, p.252], the fear of wild animals in the beginning of a civilization, which might all be considered as an **external passion**, are said to be the reasons that brought people together.
